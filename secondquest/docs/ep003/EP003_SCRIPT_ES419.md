## Adaptation notes

- **Register.** Neutral Latin American, *tú* form, the same as EP002 (curly quotes “ ”, "Dímelo en los comentarios", "nuestra próxima quest"). It uses no Spain-only words (*vale, tío, vosotros, coche, mola*) and no strongly regional slang (*ahorita, de una, chévere, che*). Word choices: *bote* (not *barca*), *auto* (neutral; avoids *carro*/*coche*), *cachetada* (slap), *utilería* (props; Spain would say *atrezo*), *chat del grupo / chat de grupo*.
- **Title.** "How to Fish" stays in English. In **l47** the gloss “cómo pescar” is added once ("How to Fish, “cómo pescar”, suena a tutorial"), because the payoff (l48–l50) only works if the viewer knows the title means "how to fish". It adds 9 characters to a line that is still at 1.17.
- **Payoff, l50.** "Cómo decir “te extraño”... sin tener que decirlo nunca." *Cómo* echoes the glossed title. *Te extraño* is the standard LatAm form (Spain says *te echo de menos*). Ending on *nunca* gives a soft, final cadence.
- **Money.** "unos ocho dólares" (l04). After that it is just "ocho dólares", as in the English.
- **Group-chat lines.** "It's eight dollars. Just get it." → “Son ocho dólares. Cómpralo y ya.” ("y ya" is the natural pushy-friend tone). "I'll get it later" → “Después lo compro” (*después* rather than *luego*, which in Mexico can sound like "right away"). "Bought." → "Comprado." (literal, so it matches any on-screen chat text).
- **Dialogue (l13–l16).** Adapted to how Latin Americans actually make plans that die: “Tenemos que vernos.” / “¡Sí! ¡Claro que sí!” / “Yo puedo en tres semanas.” / “Listo, tres semanas.”
- **l17.** Rebuilt with the Spanish dative ("a alguien se le enferma el hijo", "al auto de otro le sale un ruido nuevo"), which sounds much more lived-in than a literal "el hijo de alguien está enfermo".
- **"asking" (l18–l20).** Rendered as *pedirlo* everywhere ("Es pedirlo." / "Ocho dólares te ahorran pedirlo."), so the idea carries across the three lines.
- **l28.** "And then it gets rid of it almost immediately" → "Y casi de inmediato, lo tira por la borda." This adds a boat idiom ("throws it overboard"). It is shorter than the English and fits the fishing frame.
- **Steam quote (l29).** Translated faithfully and kept as a quote, with "textualmente" for "word for word": “violencia caricaturesca contra peces y apuestas con peces... (sin dinero real)”. If the Producer prefers showing the original English on screen, the narration still works as a translation of it.
- **Friendslop (l36–l37).** The word is kept. l36 explains it the way the English does (cheap, chaotic co-op games). l37 makes the meaning of *slop* explicit for Spanish speakers: "Bautizamos un género con una palabra que significa basura. Y luego invitamos a nuestros amigos."
- **l38.** "this year's summer hit" → "el éxito de este verano". The meaning is "the season's fad"; the summer-hit concept is understood across LatAm even where July is winter.
- **l41.** "last round" → “la última y ya”, the idiomatic late-night version. "Nunca es la última." keeps the deadpan.
- **l42.** "high school" → "la escuela", because *preparatoria / secundaria / colegio* mean different school levels in different countries. "first paid game" → "primer juego comercial", which avoids *de pago* (Spain) and *pago* (Southern Cone).
- **l05–l06.** "Millions of people" → "Millones de jugadores", so that the standalone "Juntos." agrees grammatically ("personas" would require "Juntas").
- **l51.** "what game deserves a 'why?' next" → "¿Y qué juego merece ser nuestro próximo “por qué”?" (EP002 wording).
