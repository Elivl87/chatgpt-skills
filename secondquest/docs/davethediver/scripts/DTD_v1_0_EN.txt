SECONDQUEST — Dave the Diver
EN DRAFT v1.0 — NOT LOCKED (2026-10-18)
Title A: Dave the Diver: Why Millions Work Two Jobs... to Relax
Thesis: Dave's work is everything ours isn't: it ends, you can see it, and someone is glad you came.

[A · HOOK]
¶01 You finish work. You get home. You sit down.
¶02 And you start a second job.
¶03 By day, you dive into a hole in the ocean and spear fish until your oxygen runs out. By night, you serve them as sushi until closing time.
¶04 Nobody pays you. Nobody makes you.
¶05 Dave the Diver has sold more than ten million copies, according to the studio.
¶06 Ten million people... clocking in. After clocking out.
¶07 And Steam players voted it their award for "Sit Back and Relax."
¶08 So, why?

[B · THE TRICK]
¶09 Meet Dave. He doesn't look like a hero. Round. Glasses. A wetsuit that has seen things. Even the developers say his body isn't typical for a diver.
¶10 Dave is on vacation. Then his old friend Cobra calls, with the promise of sushi.
¶11 So Dave ends his vacation. Immediately. Because Dave really likes sushi.
¶12 There's just one detail. The restaurant has no fish. Dave is the fish supply.
¶13 One player called it "the shortest supply chain in the world." It's one guy. Holding a harpoon.
¶14 And it gets worse. Cobra keeps signing contracts for him. Without asking.
¶15 You probably have a Cobra. The friend who already told everyone you'd help them move. The boss who said yes for you, in a meeting you weren't in.
¶16 Dave could leave. He doesn't. Hold on to that.

[C · THE EASY ANSWER]
¶17 The easy answer is that it's cozy. Pixel art. Gentle music. A little sushi bar by the sea.
¶18 But it isn't only cozy. The deep has sharks. Giant crabs. A squid the size of a bus. And your oxygen is also your health. Get bitten, and the tank drops.
¶19 Danger below. Rice above.
¶20 And cozy alone doesn't explain it. Plenty of games let you relax. Ten million people didn't sign up for a nap. They signed up for a shift.

[D · THE DAY: IT ENDS]
¶21 So look at the shift.
¶22 Every morning, Dave drops into the Blue Hole. And every dive, it's different. New layout. New fish. New trouble.
¶23 And every dive has an ending you can see. A gauge. When the oxygen runs out, they pull you up, and you keep one thing. One.
¶24 Now think about your own workday. When did it last end? Not "end of day." Actually end. The email at ten at night says no.
¶25 Dave's work has a clock you can see. Yours mostly has a phone.
¶26 And when Dave gets better, you can see that too. His air tank goes from ninety bar to four hundred and eighty. His cargo box, from nine kilos to a hundred and eighty-five. His suit, from forty meters deep to eight hundred.
¶27 He doesn't get a promotion. He gets a bigger tank.
¶28 Out here, getting better at your job mostly means... more job.

[E · THE NIGHT: YOU CAN SEE IT]
¶29 Then the sun goes down, and the second half begins. Bancho Sushi.
¶30 Bancho is the chef. A man so serious about fish that, according to the game, a five-star hotel once fired him for serving a food critic a whole roasted shark head.
¶31 Every new recipe gets its own anime cutscene. Knives flash. A petal falls. Somewhere, a catfish looks terrified.
¶32 And Dave runs the floor. Tea. Wasabi. Plates. Faster, faster. Until closing. And then the game gives you a score for the night.
¶33 Here's the part that matters. The fish you speared at noon... is on a plate at eight. And somebody eats it.
¶34 Most of us make things nobody ever sees. A report goes into a folder. The folder goes into another folder.
¶35 The director actually split the game in two for this. He wanted you to feel like you were "preparing dinner for customers."
¶36 Not a task. A dinner. For someone.

[F · THE PEOPLE: SOMEONE IS GLAD YOU CAME]
¶37 And the customers aren't the only ones waiting.
¶38 Duff, the weapons guy, who loves his anime idols louder than any adult is supposed to love anything.
¶39 Otto, the farmer with the straw hat, who disappeared from his son's life and wants to fix himself before he calls home.
¶40 And deep under the Blue Hole, a whole village of Sea People, who need Dave too.
¶41 Every one of them needs Dave to do the one thing they can't.
¶42 Being needed by four thousand people is called a job. Being needed by five is called a family. Or, apparently, a sushi bar.
¶43 And yes, some players say the game never stops handing you chores. A fish farm. A rice paddy. Seahorse racing. Fair. Dave never says no either.
¶44 Which brings us back to Cobra. Dave could leave. He never does. Because somewhere along the way, the contracts became friends.

[G · THE ANSWER]
¶45 So... why do millions of people come home from work to work two jobs?
¶46 Because it was never about working more. It's about work that finally fits in a day.
¶47 It ends. You can see it. And someone is glad you came.
¶48 The idea came from a real restaurant on Jeju Island, in South Korea. In the director's words, "the owner caught fish in the morning, and cooked them for dinner."
¶49 One person. One day. A job you can hold in your hands.
¶50 Then you put the controller down. Tomorrow, the real job is still there. Still no oxygen gauge. Still no score at closing time.
¶51 But tonight, for an hour, you got to clock out like Dave. Done by closing time. Somebody glad you came.
¶52 Maybe that's what we're really playing. Not a second job. The first one... the way it was supposed to feel.
