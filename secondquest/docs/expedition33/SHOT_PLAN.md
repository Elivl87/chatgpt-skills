## The visual idea: a clair-obscur paper theatre (free, safer, on theme)
The game's title is the technique: **light against dark**. The cast of the game (Gustave, Sophie, Lune, Maelle, Verso,
the old man, Aline, Renoir, Alicia, Sciel, Monoco) is shown as **our own backlit paper silhouettes** (cut-outs built in code/props3d,
one warm rim light, one colour accent each: Gustave a brass glint on the arm and the journal, Sophie a red scarf,
Maelle a red ribbon, Verso a white streak, the old man white hair and a cane, Aline a paint-stained hand). They move
only by cutting between real poses or by light (never gliding a static pose). Faces are never the game's faces.
**Quest**, in an expedition coat (identity kept), is the player and the viewer's face: he carries the emotion in
close-ups, watches, walks the world, and is the thumbnail. Why: (1) emotion through posture and light reads stronger
than a copied face; (2) it avoids "visuals matching third-party content" now that no rights request is made; (3) the
cast costs 0 credits; (4) at the reveal ("the world is a painting") the paper theatre literally becomes a canvas.

puzzled, squashed-shy, content (eyes closed), wet-eyed smile, asleep. Child's sketch of him pinned by the easel in E.
