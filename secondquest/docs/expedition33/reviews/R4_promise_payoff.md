# R4 · Promise, rights, voice and payoff · A_v2 (independent review, 2026-10-17)

Lens: title/thumbnail promise, rights risk, channel voice, the motto's final appearance. I did not edit the script.

**Score: 8/10.** The promise lands early and the payoff is built properly. The risk is outside the narration: the
chapters, the thumbnail art, and how long the most recognisable cue plays.

## Verdict by question
- **Promise, and how early:** delivered. "Losing everyone" arrives at ¶03 (~0:12) and "ONE LAST MINUTE" at ¶04
  (~0:18). Frame 0 is the thumbnail. The title is a premise statement, not a twist, so it spoils nothing.
- **Spoiler leaks:** the title, thumbnail and hook are clean. **The leak is the description's chapters.** If they are
  generated from the block headers (PUBLISHING_STANDARD §4.3), "A mother with a brush", "Two siblings" and "The mission
  works" spoil the pivot. They would show in the description, on the scrubber and as search "key moments".
- **The motto:** the setup works. The question (¶32) is restated as "his last words were a question" (¶88), then
  2.5 s of silence, then the same words on the page as a statement. This completes the argument and needs no
  explanation. One thing weakens it: ¶84 states the moral ahead of time.
- **The endings:** close to balanced (mirrored ¶72, the rock verdict ¶74, ¶82 naming both doors). There is one tilt.
  ¶81 casts Aline's "stopped everything" as the failure, and Maelle's door literally continues Aline's act. The
  "morning after" imagery (Paris morning, the grave, waking up) also belongs to Verso's door. Together these quietly
  crown it.
- **Voice:** mostly SecondQuest. Lines like "recruitment poster", "We'll wait. Probably.", "You were fighting a
  marriage" and "Not a metaphor. An actual painting." are on target. Block I and ¶84 drift into generic essay speech.
  The average sentence is 5.8 words against a 6.5–8 target. That is acceptable here, but don't add more fragments.
- **The music map:** it serves the structure well. The beach, the death, the reveal and the choice are dry, and the
  motto comes after silence.
  - **Highest exposure:** "Une vie à t'aimer" from ¶52 to ¶65, about 75–90 s continuous. It is the most recognisable,
    vocal-led cue and swells to full in the rests, which gives Content ID its strongest fingerprint.
  - **Next:** "For Those Who Come After" from ¶79 to ¶88, about 50 s. It is a Laced Records licensed release, so a
    label-side claim is plausible.
  - **The fallback makes this worse:** it brings "Une vie" back at J and doubles exposure on the same track.
  - **Safest minimal-music version:** the whole video dry, except one excerpt of ≤ 25 s of "Une vie à t'aimer" on
    ¶52–¶53. That is the reinterpreted fight, the most value per second. Drop "Lumière". Keep the motto dry. Upload
    private and read Checks. If it is blocked, go fully dry: the script works that way.
