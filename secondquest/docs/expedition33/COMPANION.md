# Expedition 33 · Companion detail (RULE, Producer 2026-10-11)

Navi (EP002) and the unimpressed fish (EP003) set the bar: a small companion from the game's world, present through
the video, never stealing a line, **interacting with the wordmark** at the hook's "So, why?" and at the end. Test:
(1) reads as the game's world; (2) carries the thesis or a running gag; (3) can move into the wordmark; (4) cost;
(5) has an expression set (fish v2 is the bar).

**Update 2026-10-17:** the Producer suggests **Esquie**, as Navi was for Zelda. He also wants **faithful art** for this
episode, pending the policy check (RESEARCH §7b). Esquie is evaluated first, with both versions; the earlier options
stay as fallbacks.

## Who Esquie is (verified, RESEARCH §3c)
- A huge, round, gentle creature who becomes the party's **companion and mount** on the world map. Lumière thinks of him
  as a myth from children's stories. He asks the party to recover the stones that power his abilities (swimming, later
  diving, and so on). **Verso is an old friend of his.** (Wikipedia-derived wiki; finalboss.io; playnews.gg)
- **Designed as a "lovable counterweight" to the bleak story.** Sandfall drew on Chien-Po (*Mulan*); the voice was
  shaped toward Baymax's gentleness ("cette volonté pure de prendre soin des autres"). (millenium.org, 29 Jan 2026)
- **Spoiler:** **the real Verso created the canvas and "used it as a place to play, paint, and imagine fictional
  entities like Esquie, the Gestrals, and others."** (Destructoid, ending explained.) Esquie is the dead boy's
  imagination, still alive inside his painting.
- **Verso's ending:** the canvas is destroyed and "the painted beings are lost with it", so the family moves on "at the
  loss of everyone inside the Canvas" (Destructoid). Esquie's own fate in either ending is **not described** in our
  sources: **UNVERIFIED, so the narration never states it.**
- **A fan icon:** Sandfall had to warn players about **counterfeit Esquie plushes** and promised an official one
  (millenium.org, 23 May 2025). Voice credit: Maxence Cazorla (Wikipedia cast list); one article names someone
  else, so recheck.

## Esquie, evaluated honestly

**1. Fit with the argument (grief, letting go, the painted world).** It is excellent, and better after the spoiler.
- Before the warning (hook and early blocks), Esquie is simply the world's gentle giant. The hook does not explain who
  he is, so there is **no spoiler**: non-players see a big, kind creature with Quest.
- After the reveal, the audience realises he was **imagined by the son who died**. Every scene with him becomes a scene
  with something the lost person made. That is package A's thesis ("what do we do with what they left?") and package
  C's ("the painting a son made"). In B he is the lightness that makes a deadline bearable.
- **Spoiler discipline:** the narration never says "Verso created Esquie" before the warning card. In package A it can
  be shown, not said: in block D, as the camera pulls back to the canvas, a child's sketch of Esquie is pinned beside
  the easel (our drawing). The audience connects it, and no narration is changed.

**2. Comic and tender range.** It is wide; Chien-Po and Baymax are the right lineage. Gags that never steal a line:
- *Comic:* he can't fit through the harbor gate and goes round; he tries to read the "muscles" journal upside down and
  copies the pose; he hides behind Quest from the giant brush, which hides nothing; he sits on the café napkin (B) or the
  battle menu (C) and squashes it; the monster's polite wait in C turns into Esquie waiting even more politely.
- *Tender:* he puts a huge hand on Quest's shoulder on the cliff (off-frame of the death, after it); in "four ways to
  grieve" (A) he sits on the floor beside the four chairs, too big for any of them; at the choice he looks at both
  doors and doesn't pick.
- *Expression set needed:* beaming, puzzled, squashed-shy, eyes closed (content), wet-eyed smile, asleep.

**3. Wordmark interactions (Navi flew into the star of the "o").**
- **Hook, "So, why?":** Esquie rises behind the wordmark like a sunrise, then flops forward onto it. His round body
  **becomes the "o"** (he is the right shape). The gold bar lands under him; he beams; 1.5 s hold. A bounce, not a flight:
  his own physics.
- **End (book-end):** the same "o", but **empty**. Esquie walks up, looks at it, and then gives the "o" to the paint
  stroke (gold), smiling, and steps out of frame. What a lost person made is let go, and the mark stays. (For a lighter
  ending, he settles into the "o" and falls asleep. Ask the Producer which tone he wants.)

**4. Motto arc / final beat: can he carry it without fan service?** **Yes, if he is a witness and never the speaker.**
- He never "says" the motto, never holds a sign, never gets a hero shot on the line.
- **Final beat (all packages):** the last appearance of FOR THOSE WHO COME AFTER is unchanged (Bram, alone, after
  silence). Esquie's role is **one quiet gesture before it**. A: inside the canvas, before the cloth falls, Esquie waves,
  small and happy, and the cloth covers him. B: he stays on the harbor and watches the girl walk back into the city;
  he doesn't follow. C: he sits beside the covered painting with his eyes closed; when the new canvas appears he opens
  them. The gesture is about letting go, so he serves the argument rather than decorating it.
- **Risk of fan service:** if Esquie appears on the payoff line itself, or does a catchphrase, it becomes a cameo.
  The rule is that he moves only in the rests, and he leaves the frame before the motto.

**Clear reasons against?** None on story or craft. The **only** real argument against is IP: Esquie is the single most
protected character in the game, merch-wise. That does not overrule the Producer's choice for a personal,
non-monetized episode, but it is the item for the coordinator's risk check. **If the check says no, the paint drop
(Option A) is ready and costs nothing.**
