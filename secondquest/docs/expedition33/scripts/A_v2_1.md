## Creative spine (unchanged from v2)
- **Viewer question:** Why do millions of people love a game that takes away almost everyone you meet?
- **Hidden question:** How do you love someone who's gone without stopping the lives of the people still here?
- **Compass, never narrated as a list:** what we owe the dead · what we owe the living · what we owe those who come after.
- **Thesis:** Expedition 33 keeps changing what "saving" means until the only thing left worth saving is what people
  leave behind. Gustave knew it on page one of his journal. The Dessendres take a whole game to learn it, and so do you.
- **Payoff:** Gustave's last words are a *question* ("For those who come after, right?"). The video's last line is the
  same words as an answer. "Those who come after" are now everyone who wakes up the next morning, in either ending,
  and the viewer.

### A · Hook · spoiler-free

> FRAME 0 = the thumbnail, live: Quest's close-up face, eyes brimming, the black tower over his shoulder with the "33" being painted stroke by stroke, a petal lifting off his palm. **ONE LAST MINUTE on screen 0:00–0:02** (the thumbnail's words, then they fade). The voice names the man we are looking at in line 1 (R1). Pull back to the dusk harbor: a woman seen from behind; far across the water, a huge round shape watches the tower (Esquie, unexplained).

**¶01** This man gets one last minute with the woman he loves.

**¶02** Because tomorrow, a giant woman paints thirty-three on that tower.

**¶03** And everyone thirty-three or older... turns to petals.

**¶04** She's thirty-three.

**¶05** That's how this game begins. Not with a tutorial. With a goodbye.

**¶06** Game of the Year at every major show. Eight million copies.

**¶07** Millions of people paid... to lose everyone.

**¶08** So, why?

> The wordmark lands with its gold bar; Esquie rises behind it, flops forward and settles into the "o", beaming. 1.5 s of silence.


### W · Warning (the FULL SPOILERS card plays under this line)

> The card FULL SPOILERS · INCLUDING BOTH ENDINGS sits under the whole line (no separate hold, R1).

**¶09** One warning. The answer is in the ending, so we're talking about all of it. Never played it? Stay. You'll see why people cried. Still playing? Come back when you're done. We'll wait. Probably.


### B · The city that's used to it · step 1: Lumière, the Gommage, a clear enemy

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 0:40): a Lumière street in the morning. Cafés, lamps, long coats, no grey hair anywhere: an empty bench. Then Sophie's face, calm.

**¶10** Lumière. Paris, around nineteen hundred. Iron lamps. Cafés. Long coats.

**¶11** And not a single grey hair.

**¶12** No grandparents. In Lumière, the oldest person you know is always the next one to go.

**¶13** The woman is Sophie. She isn't angry. She says, word for word: "I've lived 33 good years. Look around."

**¶14** That's the strange part. The city isn't panicking. It even has a saying for it. "Tomorrow comes."

**¶15** And it has an enemy. The Paintress. Kill her, and the number stops.

**¶16** So every year, the ones with one year left sail out to try. Every expedition before this one has failed.

**¶17** Which is not a great line for the recruitment poster.


### C · Gustave's journal · step 2: Gustave, sacrifice, the motto's first meaning

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 1:25): the beach. Gustave's face as the expedition is cut down: fear, not heroics. Then the night camp; Lune holds his stare.

**¶18** The man from the opening is Gustave. An engineer. Thirty-two. One year left, and he's spending it on the boat.

**¶20** The expedition lands. Within minutes, almost all of it is gone. Gustave falls apart. He tells Lune, a survivor, he's done.

**¶21** And Lune reminds him of their oath. "When one falls, we continue." Then she leans on one word. "Not if. When."

**¶22** It's not a promise that you'll survive. It's a promise that you won't be the end.

> Esquie: huge, round, beaming; he tries to fit through a gap he will clearly not fit through.

**¶23** So he keeps walking. And the survivors meet Esquie.

**¶24** A creature out of Lumière's old stories. Enormous, gentle, missing his magic rocks, and perfecting, in his words, "the Art... of Napping."

> The journal page, close: his handwriting, the ink still wet on the sign-off. The words appear on the page, not painted over the image.

**¶25** At night, Gustave writes in his journal. The monsters. The beauty of it all. It's for his apprentices back home.

**¶26** And he signs it off the same way.

**¶27** "For those who come after."

**¶28** That's what it means at the start. Not "we'll win." Something quieter. I don't need to live forever for my life to matter.


### D · The cliff and the rock · step 3: the mission, Gustave's death, life that goes on

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 2:35): the old man with white hair on a ridge, closer each time. On the cliff we cut before any blow: Gustave in front of Maelle; his face; her face. Then 2 s of silence.

**¶29** And an old man with white hair keeps getting in their way.

**¶31** At the end of the first act, he catches them on a cliff. Gustave steps in front of Maelle, sixteen, the girl he and his sister Emma took in... and he doesn't get back up.

**¶32** His last words are to her. And they're a question. "For those who come after, right?"

**¶33** And the old man says something that makes no sense yet. "You won't yet understand. But this is a kindness, not a cruelty."

**¶34** Hold on to that.

> The funeral: Maelle's face, Lune's, Sciel's. Esquie sits a little apart, too big for the circle.

**¶35** At the funeral, Lune says the oath again. "When one falls, we continue."

**¶36** So you continue, with a stranger and his sword. His name is Verso.

**¶37** And Esquie, Verso's old friend, sums it up. "Well, the super nice one died but... the others are cool too."

**¶38** Then he tells Verso: "Losing a rock is better than never having a rock. Ask me how I know."

**¶39** Verso says: "Debatable."

**¶40** Remember that argument. The whole ending is that argument.

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 3:45): camp at night. Verso and Maelle by the fire; he mimes playing on his knee; she points across the dark toward where the opera house would be. 2 s breath after the line.

**¶41** And there's still life. At camp, Verso admits he studied piano. Maelle has a plan: when this is over, they fix up the opera house, and he plays for everyone.


### E · The mission works · step 4 (certainty) → step 6 (the reveal, the retention pivot)

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 4:00): the climb up the tower; the old man guarding the Paintress. Then silence, and the camera pulls back from Lumière... and back... until the city is a canvas on an easel in a dim Paris studio around 1900. A covered mirror. A child's sketch of Esquie pinned beside the easel.

**¶42** So you push on. Up the tower. The old man is guarding the Paintress. You beat him. You beat her.

**¶43** The mission Gustave died for... worked.

**¶44** And then the game takes it back.

**¶45** The Paintress is gone, and the Gommage doesn't stop. It takes everyone in Lumière. Everyone.

**¶46** Because she was never the one painting death. She was the one holding it back.

**¶47** And the world itself is a painting. Not a metaphor. An actual painting.

**¶48** A family of painters, the Dessendres, can paint worlds... and walk into them. Their son, Verso, painted this one. Then there was a fire. And Verso died.

> A beat on the playable Verso's face, then cut. (Open loop, paid by ¶63.)

**¶48b** So who's been carrying your sword?


### F · A mother with a brush · step 7 (Aline) + step 5 (Renoir, three readings)

> Aline in close-up: a mother, not a monster; no villain lighting.

**¶49** His mother, Aline, went to the last place her son still was. She walked into his painting. And she didn't come out.

**¶50** She became the Paintress. And inside, she painted her family back. A son. A husband.

> The light beat after 4:00 (R2): the child's Esquie sketch again, a crayon rock beside it. Hold 1 s on it before ¶51.

**¶50b** And Esquie? The rocks, the napping? A little boy dreamed him up.

**¶51** Call it madness. Then think of the room you still haven't cleared out. Aline just had a brush.

> **The only default music cue:** "Une vie à t'aimer", one excerpt ≤ 25 s, under ¶52–¶53 (see Music map).

**¶52** And the old man on the cliff... was a painted husband. Made by a grieving woman to stand between her and anyone who'd take this away.

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 5:15): back to the tower fight, seen now as a man shielding his wife.

**¶53** Go back to that fight now. You weren't fighting a monster. You were fighting a marriage.

> Renoir, the real father: a tired face, not a monster's. His line is played straight, not ominous.

**¶54** And the real father, Renoir, came in to bring her home. In his words: "For the sake of the living, we must part with the dead." So he erases. And every year, she holds him back a little less.

**¶55** That's the number on the tower. That's Sophie at the harbor.

**¶57** To save the family he still had, he was destroying what was left of the son he lost.

**¶58** Nobody in this family thinks they're the villain. Even the man on the cliff called it a kindness.


### G · Two siblings · step 8: Verso and Maelle, equal arguments

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 6:00): Maelle's face, then Verso's, framed identically (same lens, light, length on screen).

**¶59** And then it gets closer.

**¶60** Maelle, the girl Gustave died for, is Alicia. The little sister from the fire. Reborn in the painting, with no memory.

**¶61** Out there, she's a scarred girl in a house full of grief. In here, she had Gustave. Friends. A life.

**¶62** Her case isn't selfish. At the very end, she'll say it: "This lifetime that was stolen from us."

**¶63** And Verso isn't the real son. He's a painted one, held together by the last piece of the real one. And that piece wants to stop.

**¶64** His answer is even shorter. "I don't want this life."

**¶65** One wants to stay where she finally belongs. One is begging to be allowed to end. Both are right about something.


### H · The brush · step 9: the two endings, mirrored

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 6:50): the brush held out to the camera, to the viewer. Two doors. Each door gets the same framing and roughly the same time (Maelle 48 words, Verso 44).

**¶66** At the end, you choose.

> Door one: Lumière rebuilt, faces coming home; then the opera house, Verso at the piano, the audience; then Maelle's face, changing as she stays. Beautiful and wrong in one image.

**¶67** Maelle's way. The painting stays. Lumière is rebuilt. Its people come back. Gustave. Sophie. Everyone the petals took... home.

**¶69** And the opera house. Everyone's there. Verso plays. But she painted him back for it. He never asked. And the longer she stays, the more it costs her.

> Door two: the canvas fading; Lune's stare; Sciel; Monoco. Then Paris, morning: a family together (the grave: recheck in-game, see notes).

**¶70** Verso's way. The painting is erased, and everyone in it goes too. Lune. Sciel. Monoco. Every camp. Every song. In Paris, a family finally stands together at a grave.

**¶71** Because a whole world is gone. Thirty hours of it. Nobody ever plays that opera house.

**¶72** One way saves the world, and costs the girl who's still alive. The other saves the girl, and costs the world.

**¶73** The game won't tell you which one is right. People still argue.

> Esquie and Verso at camp, from ¶38–¶39, replayed small and silent in a corner of the frame.

**¶74** Which is just Esquie and Verso again. "Losing a rock is better than never having a rock." "Debatable."


### J · Tomorrow comes · steps 10 + 11: what "saving" means → legacy (blocks I and J of v2 merged, R2)

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 7:50): Emma's farewell podium in Lumière, empty.

**¶75** The story started with one simple sentence. We have to save this world.

**¶76** By the end, you're not sure what saving is.

**¶78** Nobody here is wrong to love. The only question is what that love is allowed to stop.

**¶79** And that's where Gustave was ahead of all of them.

**¶80** He never tried to keep anything. He wrote it down, for kids he'd never see again.

**¶81** Aline tried to keep a person. Gustave tried to keep a promise.

> Cut between two mornings, same length each (R4): Lumière rebuilt at dawn, then Paris at dawn. Neither door owns the morning after.

**¶82** Whichever door you pick, somebody wakes up the next morning. Maelle, in her painting. A family, in Paris. You, after the credits.

**¶83** Lumière already had the words. "Tomorrow comes."

> VISUAL BEAT (≈ 8:30): Gustave's journal on a table in morning light, closed.

**¶84** A journal. A song at a piano. A rock you were lucky to have.

**¶85** You don't forget them. You just don't disappear with them.

**¶86** So... why do millions of people love a game about losing everyone?

**¶87** Because it was never really about losing them. It's about the morning after.

**¶88** A man spent his last minute at a harbor. His last words were a question, to a girl who had to go on without him.
